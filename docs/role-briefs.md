# Role Briefs

## Central Coordinator / Facilitator

**Position:** Indonesian central government

**Objectives**

- strengthen national control over nickel resources
- preserve downstream industrial development
- distribute adjustment costs without destabilizing the coalition
- produce an implementable policy package

**Core constraints**

- limited fiscal and administrative capacity
- dependence on foreign capital and technology
- local implementation gaps
- trade and ESG pressure

**Facilitator tasks**

- present the draft clauses
- ask each stakeholder to state interests and conditions
- identify which commitments are required
- verify resources, authority, and dependencies
- decide whether to adopt, revise, or maintain the baseline

The facilitator should not treat verbal support as sufficient implementation.

## Mining Companies

**Position:** IUP/IUPK holders and independent mining operators

**Primary interests**

- stable production and sales
- predictable permit and quota rules
- recovery of sunk investment
- ability to adjust contracts without excessive penalties

**Likely demands**

- phased quota reduction
- temporary relief or compensation
- transparent revision rules
- protection from arbitrary enforcement

**Opening statement**

> We accept the need for better resource governance, but a rapid quota cut would break contracts and strand investment. We need a credible transition schedule and clear rules for revising this year's allocation.

**Main trade-off**

The company may accept a tighter long-term quota if it receives a predictable transition path and protection from sudden cancellation.

## Smelters and Downstream Industries

**Position:** processing, smelting, and battery-material operators

**Primary interests**

- stable ore quality and volume
- high utilization of existing capacity
- predictable pricing and logistics
- continued access to export markets

**Likely demands**

- guaranteed ore allocation
- coordinated scheduling with mining quotas
- stable HPM and tax treatment
- time to adapt before new compliance conditions take effect

**Opening statement**

> Our plants cannot operate without reliable ore. If the government tightens the quota, it must coordinate the timing with existing smelting capacity and avoid creating an avoidable shutdown.

**Main trade-off**

The sector may support downstream deepening if supply continuity and market access remain credible.

## Local Regional Governments

**Position:** provincial and district authorities in producing regions

**Primary interests**

- local fiscal revenue
- employment and household income
- public services and infrastructure
- environmental and safety supervision

**Likely demands**

- compensation for lost local revenue
- earmarked funds for public services
- clearer authority and adequate staffing
- local participation in monitoring and transition planning

**Opening statement**

> Mining supports our budget and public services. A quota reduction cannot leave us with additional enforcement duties but fewer resources. Transition support must reach the producing regions.

**Main trade-off**

Local governments can help implement a credible plan, but only if duties are matched with resources and political support.

## Affected Local Communities

**Position:** workers, residents, Indigenous groups, fishers, farmers, and environmental interests

**Primary interests**

- employment and livelihood security
- clean water, air, land, and coastal ecosystems
- health and safety
- meaningful consultation and grievance mechanisms
- fair compensation and environmental restoration

**Likely demands**

- no unilateral reduction in jobs or services
- independent environmental monitoring
- community participation in project and transition decisions
- enforceable compensation and remediation commitments

**Opening statement**

> We are not a single interest group. Some residents depend on mining jobs, while others bear the costs of pollution, land loss, and disrupted livelihoods. Decisions require differentiated impacts, transparent information, and accessible complaint channels.

**Main trade-off**

Communities may accept industrial adjustment only if employment, health, environmental, and procedural safeguards are credible.
