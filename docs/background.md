# Background Briefing

## 1. Why Nickel Matters

Nickel is a strategic input for stainless steel and lithium-ion batteries. The global energy transition has increased demand for nickel-bearing battery materials, while supply remains geographically concentrated. Indonesia is the central case: public 2024 estimates place it at roughly 62.5% of mined nickel production and China at roughly 57% of refined nickel consumption.

Indonesia has used this position to move beyond raw-ore exports. The 2020 ore export ban, later licensing rules, the HPM pricing formula, state-owned capital, and annual RKAB production approvals are all part of an effort to retain smelting, investment, fiscal revenue, and downstream industrial capacity inside the country.

This strategy creates power, but also vulnerability. Indonesia depends on foreign processing capital, technology, export markets, and continued investment. It also faces environmental, trade, and market-access rules from the European Union, the United States, and other partners.

## 2. The Policy Decision

The simulated decision concerns the tightening of Indonesia's nickel RKAB production quota.

The government wants to:

- slow resource depletion
- improve control over mining volumes and revenue distribution
- support downstream industrialization
- strengthen Indonesia's position in the global battery supply chain

A rapid quota reduction can instead produce:

- disrupted mining contracts and reduced investment recovery
- insufficient ore for existing smelters
- lower local government revenue
- lost jobs and higher living costs in producing regions
- environmental and compensation conflicts

The policy problem is therefore not simply "tighten or do not tighten." It is a negotiation over the pace, scope, compensation, and credibility of adjustment.

## 3. Timeline

| Period | Development | Relevance to the simulation |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | Indonesia bans nickel-ore exports | Shifts policy from raw-material exports toward domestic processing |
| 2022 | LME nickel prices experience extreme dislocation | Shows that global finance, inventories, and geopolitics also shape prices |
| 2024 | Indonesia completes a political transition | Changes coalition management and policy style while retaining resource-nationalist goals |
| 2026 | RKAB quota approvals tighten | Creates immediate tension among miners, smelters, local governments, and communities |

The 2022 price spike must not be treated as evidence that the Indonesian export ban alone caused the global nickel price increase. Low inventories, the Russia-Ukraine war, and financial-market stress were also important.

## 4. Stakeholders

### Central Coordinator

Represents the national government. Sets the policy direction, proposes the package, coordinates compensation, and decides whether to adopt, revise, or maintain the baseline.

### Mining Companies

Need predictable production rights, sales contracts, and investment recovery. They prefer a longer transition and clear rules for revising quota allocations.

### Smelters and Downstream Industries

Need continuous ore supply for facilities that have already been built. They care about capacity utilization, input costs, and the credibility of long-term supply commitments.

### Local Governments

Depend on mining for fiscal revenue and employment. They need resources for public services, permitting, environmental supervision, and local economic diversification.

### Affected Communities

Depend partly on mining employment but also bear environmental, health, land, and livelihood risks. They need credible consultation, compensation, environmental remediation, and accessible grievance channels.

## 5. Core Negotiation Trade-offs

1. **Quota magnitude**: How fast should production be reduced?
2. **Transition costs**: Who pays for suspended contracts, unused capacity, and temporary unemployment?
3. **Transformation and public services**: How will training, local revenue, and community services be funded?

## 6. Evidence Boundary

The simulation uses public regulations, company disclosures, public research, and coded relationships. It does not use confidential government documents, interviews, or internal corporate data. Stakeholder preferences are aggregated analytical constructs, not authorized statements by real organizations.
