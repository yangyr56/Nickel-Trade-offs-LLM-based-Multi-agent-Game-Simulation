# Teaching Guide: Neoclassical Realism

## 1. Core Proposition

Neoclassical realism argues that international structure provides opportunities and constraints, but does not mechanically determine foreign or resource policy. Domestic intervening variables shape how national leaders interpret the external environment and what policies they can implement.

## 2. Causal Chain

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A[International structural opportunities and constraints] --> B[Domestic political filtering]
  B --> C[Strategic perception]
  C --> D[State capacity and coalition support]
  D --> E[National policy output]
  E --> F[Domestic and international feedback]
  F --> A
```

## 3. Variables in the Simulation

### International structural variables

- nickel demand created by the energy transition
- dependence on foreign processing capital and technology
- ESG, carbon-border, and market-access rules
- trade disputes and alternative supply

### Domestic intervening variables

- presidential priorities and strategic perception
- coalition relations with state-owned enterprises and local governments
- implementation authority and fiscal capacity
- bargaining with firms and affected communities

### Policy output

- quota adjustment pace
- transition financial support
- training and local-capacity measures
- public-service and community safeguards

### Feedback

- investment confidence
- supply continuity
- compliance and market access
- local legitimacy and protest

## 4. Questions for Classroom Analysis

1. Which international constraint is most important in the scenario?
2. How do leaders' beliefs change the interpretation of the same structural pressure?
3. Which domestic actor can veto or delay implementation?
4. Does a policy with majority support also have sufficient resources and cooperation?
5. When does external dependence become political vulnerability?

## 5. Common Misinterpretations

- **Determinism:** International pressure does not automatically dictate policy.
- **Presidential dominance:** Formal authority does not guarantee local implementation.
- **Stakeholder harmony:** Aggregate national gains do not show how costs are distributed.
- **AI authority:** LLM statements are simulated bargaining text, not evidence of real preferences.
- **Single-cause pricing:** Nickel prices respond to global finance, inventories, demand, and geopolitics as well as Indonesian policy.

## 6. Debrief Structure

1. Ask each actor to explain what it gained and what it conceded.
2. Compare the adopted resolution with the unilateral baseline.
3. Identify which conditions were unverifiable or unresolved.
4. Ask whether the outcome supports or challenges neoclassical realism.
5. Separate model logic, historical evidence, and normative judgment.

## 7. Assessment Rubric

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Theoretical reasoning | Connects systemic pressure to domestic mediation and policy |
| Evidence use | Distinguishes laws, company disclosures, research, and assumptions |
| Bargaining quality | Identifies interests, conditions, and trade-offs |
| Implementation analysis | Checks authority, resources, and necessary cooperation |
| Reflexivity | States uncertainty and avoids overclaiming |
